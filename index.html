Our History

Cape Cod Village Club, Inc. was formed in 1939, but the history of the land it sits on stretches back much further.

Early documentation and historic accounts show that James Caldwell—a notable Irish immigrant and successful entrepreneur—was provided a land patent to the land Cape Cod Village Club sits on back in 1787 as part of his venture to develop travel and commerce in the Lake George region. Even prior to colonial expansion into the area, this region of the Adirondacks—and indeed the majority of the Adirondacks altogether—were home to the Kanonsionni native people (now referred to as the Six Nations and more commonly known as the Iroquois). The lands surrounding Hague and throughout the rest of the Adirondacks are perhaps some of the only places left today that resembles the ancestral lands that these ancient tribes inhabited.

Closer to the present day, in 1958, the original owners of the cottage rentals that existed here began to sell off lots to individuals. A complicated series of property sales, subdivisions, and swaps proceeded until 1966, when all property owners became Members of Cape Cod Village Club, Inc. After a few more minor property transactions — with our most recent member joining in 2026 — we arrive at the thirty-four Member association that we have today.

Timeline milestones from George Stanford’s “The Club…A Brief History.”

1787

The James Caldwell land patent

New York State granted 490 acres along the Hague lakeshore to James Caldwell. These were ancestral Kanonsionni (Six Nations) lands.

1800s

The John Jenkins Farm

The land later included John Jenkins’s farm.

1928

Mary C. Beste inherits the farm

Mary C. Beste inherited the John Jenkins Farm.

1939

Cape Cod Village begins

Cape Cod Village Inc. bought 7.5 lakeside acres plus 8 acres west of Route 9N. It operated a 12-unit motel around a village green, with a beach and dock.

1958

Cottage lots offered for sale

Cape Cod Associates began selling cottage lots.

1963

Owners acquire the remaining holdings

Glenn Barnes and seven owners bought the remaining holdings for $45,000.

August 11, 1966

The Club’s “real beginning”

After 22 lots were sold, the common property transferred to Cape Cod Village Club, Inc. George Stanford called this the Club’s “real beginning”; all owners became members.

1975

The North beach

The Club bought the Mike Reed property for $38,000, creating the North beach.

2026

Thirty-four members

The most recent member joined. Cape Cod Village Club is a 34-member association today.

Rules and Guidelines

CCVC Rules and Guidelines

Owners and renters should abide by any Town of Hague renting regulations as well as the Cape Cod Village Club Rules and Regulations listed below. Several of the rules below apply for safety reasons, and others exist to respect to the club-owned property and members' personal property.

01

Roads and Parking

10 mph speed limit. Park in the designated areas. Do not park additional cars or trailers on the lawns (we have many water and electric lines underground that are easily damaged). Also, do not park extra cars on the edge of the roads due to traffic issues and safety reasons.

02

Playground

Children under age 10 must be supervised by an adult at all times. All toys/playground equipment must be put away after use. Treat equipment with respect so there is no damage. Playground is closed at dark so people can have a quiet evening.

03

Swimming and Beach Area

Swimming only permitted between the designated swim lines. Children under age 12 must be supervised by an adult at all times. No glass bottles or containers permitted in beach area or docks. No alcoholic beverages on the beach and docks. Do not forcefully tip or rock the swim raft (the hardware and anchor line could become damaged and the raft can break away in storms).

04

Boats and Docks

In addition to the regulations below, all persons should follow NY State, Lake George Park Commission and any other Lake George regulations. Any boat with a 10hp or more motor must have a current state registration and must also be registered on Lake George with the LGPC (they patrol heavily). You may register it for approximately $30 at Dockside Landing in Hague (5 minutes from CVC). Launch motorized boats at the Hague town dock only. Small, non-motorized boats may be launched at the designated launch in CCVC. Motorized boats/personal watercraft must be kept in your house's designated slip unless other arrangements have been made with the Dockmaster. PWCs cannot occupy deep water dock slips. PWC operators must be at least 14 years old and have a valid NYS PWC boating safety certificate. All persons on a PWC must wear a life vest. All children under age 12 must wear a life vest as well as anyone being towed behind a vessel. No boats in the swimming area. No sleeping overnight on boats. No running on, or diving off the docks. All boats/kayaks on CCVC grounds are privately owned and not for renter's use.

05

Other

No RV’s or campers, weapons of any sort, fireworks or firecrackers, dogs or any kind of pets (except ESA or Service Animals), or loud parties.

06

Owners and renters

Renters will receive Community Rules when they register. Owners renting must provide name(s) and contact info for renters, dates of stay. Owners are responsible for renters and must ensure renters are familiar with the rules. Owner responsibility extends beyond the rental period for any damage caused by renters.

      4. Watercraft Rules and Regulations

      -Renters with boats must adhere to the following NY State and Lake George Park Commission boating regulations.
      -Any boat with a 10hp or more motor must have a current state registration and must also be registered on Lake George with the LGPC (they patrol heavily). You may register it for approximately $40 at a local marina or online.
      -Only launch motorized boats at the Hague town dock All trailered boats must get inspected and “sealed” at a regional inspection facility before launching into Lake George.
      -Small, non-motorized boats that can be launched by hand may use the designated launch in CCVC.
      -If the cable and lock impede your ability to launch or land your boat, please contact the DockMaster or a Dock Committee Member.
      -If the “duck fence” is in place at the time of your launch make sure to return it to proper position.
      -Do not launch from beach areas.
      -Whips are required on North Beach and Point docks.
      -Docking equipment (i.e. Lines, Bumpers) should be adequate for the size of the boat and in good condition.
      -Motorized boats/personal water craft must be kept in your house's designated slip unless other arrangements have been made with the DockMaster.
      -PWCs cannot occupy deep water dock slips.
      -PWC operators must be at least 14 years old and have a valid NYS PWC boating safety certificate per NYS PWC regulations.
      -All persons on a PWC must wear a life vest.
      -All children under age 12 must wear a life vest as well as anyone being towed behind a vessel.
      -No boats, kayaks, SUPs in the swim area.
      -No sleeping overnight on boats.
      -Proper dock lines must be used.
      -No running on, or diving off the docks.
